bio

Short bio.

Porter Jenkins is an AI scientist and researcher with a PhD from Penn State University. He is an Assistant Professor of Computer Science at Brigham Young University (BYU), where he teaches courses on Deep Learning, Databases, and Probabilistic Machine Learning. He is currently on leave from BYU to build the future of Agentic Software at Enzy. His research has been published in leading AI conferences, including ICML, KDD, AAAI, IJCAI, WWW, and WACV.

Beyond academia, Porter has several years of experience collaborating with industry partners to apply AI technologies in real-world settings. His partnerships span both startups — Delicious AI, Enzy Technologies, and NextGen Nuclear — and enterprise organizations like Vivint Smart Home. His work bridges the gap between cutting-edge research and practical AI deployment, helping shape the future of intelligent systems.

research

What I work on.

My research sits at the intersection of probabilistic deep learning, computer vision, and real-world decision-making systems, with a unifying focus on building machine learning models that are reliable enough to act on under uncertainty.

One thread of my work develops new theory and methods for uncertainty quantification in deep regression — including the Deep Double Poisson Network (ICML 2025), which enables fully heteroscedastic count regression by parameterizing the Double Poisson distribution, and the Conditional Congruence Error (ECAI 2025), a kernel-based metric that diagnoses point-wise probabilistic fit where standard calibration measures like ECE fall short. A second, complementary thread translates these probabilistic foundations into deployed perception and decision systems, especially for physical retail: CountNet3D (WACV 2023) fuses RGB images with LiDAR point clouds to count densely packed, heavily occluded objects on store shelves, and EdgeRec3D (KDD 2024) builds on that perception layer to deliver a real-time, on-edge product assortment recommender driven by a Robust Bayesian Payoff model and spatial clustering of consumer preferences, with live A/B tests demonstrating 27–35% sales lifts.

Taken together, our work argues — and empirically shows — that principled uncertainty modeling and 3D scene understanding are not just academic concerns but the missing ingredients for trustworthy, high-impact ML deployments in the physical world.

Probabilistic deep learning

New methods and metrics for trustworthy uncertainty quantification in regression — calibration, conditional fit, and full heteroscedasticity for both continuous and count outputs.

3D perception

Fusing RGB images with LiDAR point clouds to count densely packed, occluded objects in real-world retail and inventory scenes — end-to-end regression where detection-based pipelines fail.

Real-world decision systems

Bayesian reward models and spatial clustering deployed at the edge — turning noisy 3D observations into product-assortment decisions with measurable, double-digit business impact.

selected

Selected publications.

Top-tier venues that anchor my recent research arc on uncertainty quantification, 3D perception, and probabilistic decision-making.

ICLR 2026 LLM test-time scaling

Provable and Practical In-Context Policy Optimization for Self-Improvement

Tianrun Yu, Yuxiao Yang, Zhaoyang Wang, Kaixiang Zhao, Porter Jenkins, Xuchao Zhang, Chetan Bansal, Huaxiu Yao, Weitong Zhang

A theoretical and practical framework that recasts LLM self-reflection as in-context policy optimization, proving a one-layer linear self-attention transformer can imitate a bandit policy-optimization algorithm and yielding ME-ICPO — top-tier math-reasoning accuracy at modest inference cost.

ICML 2025 uncertainty count regression

Fully Heteroscedastic Count Regression with Deep Double Poisson Networks

Spencer Young*, Porter Jenkins*, Longchao Da, Jeffrey Dotson, Hua Wei

A neural count-regression model that parameterizes the Double Poisson distribution to give discrete-output networks fully heteroscedastic aleatoric uncertainty for the first time, beating Poisson, Negative Binomial, and Gaussian baselines on accuracy, calibration, and OOD detection.

ECAI 2025 calibration kernels

Beyond Calibration: Assessing the Probabilistic Fit of Neural Regressors via Conditional Congruence

Spencer Young, Cole Edgren, Riley Sinema, Andrew Hall, Nathan Dong, Porter Jenkins

A kernel-based, point-wise diagnostic for the probabilistic fit of deep regressors that detects misspecification ECE silently misses — and unlike NLL, can be computed at any new input without needing the label.

KDD 2024 3D perception recommender deployed

Personalized Product Assortment with Real-time 3D Perception and Bayesian Payoff Estimation

Porter Jenkins, Michael Selander, J. Stockton Jenkins, Andrew Merrill, Kyle Armstrong

A real-time product-assortment recommender that uses on-edge 3D vision for sales estimation, a Robust Bayesian Payoff model to handle observation noise, and spatial clustering to personalize across stores — with live A/B tests showing 27–35% sales lifts.

AAAI 2024 offline RL Bayesian

Probabilistic Offline Policy Ranking with Approximate Bayesian Computation

Longchao Da, Porter Jenkins, Trevor Schwantes, Jeffrey Dotson, Hua Wei

A Bayesian framework for ranking candidate offline policies by their expected return distributions, enabling principled, uncertainty-aware policy selection in the offline RL setting.

WACV 2023 3D vision LiDAR

CountNet3D: A 3D Computer Vision Approach to Infer Counts of Occluded Objects

Porter Jenkins, Kyle Armstrong, Stephen Nelson, Sidd Gotad, J. Stockton Jenkins, Wade Wilkey, Tanner Watts

A regression-based 3D counting network that fuses RGB and LiDAR via novel "PointBeam" proposals to count densely packed, heavily occluded objects, outperforming detection-based 3D counters on both synthetic and real retail shelves.

AAAI 2022 offline RL retail

Bayesian Model-based Offline Reinforcement Learning for Product Allocation

Porter Jenkins, Hua Wei, J. Stockton Jenkins, Zhenhui Li

A model-based offline RL approach to the combinatorial product-allocation problem, learning a Bayesian world model that captures uncertainty over consumer demand and using it to optimize shelf-level decisions.

AAAI 2021 recommender utility

Neural Utility Functions

Porter Jenkins, Ahmad Farag, J. Stockton Jenkins, Huaxiu Yao, Suhang Wang, Zhenhui Li

A framework that learns economic utility functions with neural networks, bridging classical consumer-choice theory with modern representation learning for recommendation.
